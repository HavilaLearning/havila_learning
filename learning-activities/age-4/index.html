Parent guide

How to do each activity at home

Choose an activity to see the materials, steps, questions to ask and ways to adapt the play. Follow your child’s pace; 10 to 15 minutes may be enough.

🔤Letter hunt around the houseView instructions

Learning goal: Early literacy, visual recognition and connecting letters with words.

Materials: Paper or letter cards, a pencil, and safe items with visible letters such as books, clean packaging or labels.

How to do it: Choose a familiar letter, such as the first letter of your child’s name, and write it on a card. Walk around one room looking for that letter in books, labels and packaging. Each time you find it, say the letter and the word it appears in. Finish by drawing one of the objects you found.

Questions to ask: Which letter did we find? What sound does it make? Can you find another word that begins with the same sound?

Adaptation and safety: Start with two or three familiar letters. If your child is ready, compare uppercase and lowercase forms. Avoid packaging from hazardous products.

🔢Count and match everyday objectsView instructions

Learning goal: One-to-one counting, comparing quantities and matching numerals to groups.

Materials: Five to fifteen large buttons, blocks, large lids or safe toys; number cards from 1 to 5.

How to do it: Place five objects on the table. Touch each object as you count slowly. Show the number 3 and invite your child to make a group of three. Repeat with other numbers. Finally, combine two small groups and count the total together.

Questions to ask: How many are here? How do you know there are three? Which group has more? What happens if we add one more?

Adaptation and safety: Begin with numbers 1 to 3 and gradually move up to 5 or 10. Avoid small objects that could be swallowed; use large items and supervise.

🧩Build a repeating patternView instructions

Learning goal: Recognising sequences, predicting what comes next, attention and logical thinking.

Materials: Blocks, cups, socks or large pieces in two different colours or shapes.

How to do it: Make a simple sequence: red, blue, red, blue. Ask your child to look carefully and choose what comes next. Build another sequence together. When ready, try a three-part pattern such as red, blue, yellow and invite your child to continue it.

Questions to ask: What repeats? What comes next? How did you work it out? Can you make a pattern for me to continue?

Adaptation and safety: Start with two colours and three repeats. If that is easy, use three colours, shapes or sizes. Give your child time to explain their thinking.

🔷Shape detectiveView instructions

Learning goal: Recognising geometric shapes and developing spatial vocabulary through observation.

Materials: Paper, pencil and safe household objects such as a plate, book, box and large lid.

How to do it: Choose one shape at a time. Look around the home for objects that resemble a circle, square, triangle or rectangle. Draw the shape on paper and place a drawing or name of each object beside it. Talk about sides, corners and curves without expecting formal definitions.

Questions to ask: Where can you see a round shape? Does this object have corners? How are a square and a rectangle different?

Adaptation and safety: Start with circles and squares, then introduce triangles and rectangles. Let your child turn and examine real objects from different angles.

🔬Sink or float discoveryView instructions

Learning goal: Observation, making predictions, comparing results and early scientific thinking.

Materials: A shallow basin with a little water, a towel and large safe objects such as a sponge, plastic spoon, large lid and bath toy.

How to do it: Before placing each object in the water, ask your child what they think will happen. Test one object at a time. Observe whether it stays on the surface or sinks. Sort the objects into two groups and discuss what happened. Dry the floor and materials afterwards.

Questions to ask: What do you think will happen? What did you observe? Was your prediction right? Which object surprised you?

Adaptation and safety: Use only a little water and keep an adult within arm’s reach throughout. Empty the basin immediately. Never leave a child alone near water.

🎨Draw and tell a storyView instructions

Learning goal: Creativity, story sequence, speaking and organising ideas.

Materials: Paper and crayons or coloured pencils.

How to do it: Invite your child to draw a scene: a visit, a trip or a game. Ask them to tell you what happened first, next and at the end. Write one sentence dictated by your child below the drawing and read it aloud together.

Questions to ask: Who is in your story? Where are they? What happened first? How did it end? What might happen next?

Adaptation and safety: If your child is unsure how to begin, offer two topic choices or draw together. Value the story and ideas, not the quality of the drawing.

Learning works best when it feels natural

Talk, play, read, count, make and explore together. The goal is not to make every moment a lesson, but to notice opportunities for learning in everyday life.
