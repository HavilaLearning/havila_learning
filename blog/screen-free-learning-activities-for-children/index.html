20 Screen-Free Learning Activities for Children at Home

Simple activities using everyday materials to support literacy, maths, creativity, science, fine motor skills, problem-solving and family connection.

Children do not need expensive toys or complicated equipment to learn. A sheet of paper can become a writing activity. Bottle caps can become counting objects. A cardboard box can become a building project. A walk outside can become a science lesson.

20 screen-free learning activities

1. Count household objects

Choose safe objects and count them together. Ask which group has more or fewer.

2. Letter hunt

Choose one letter and find it on books, labels or packages. Say its sound together.

3. Storytelling from pictures

Choose a picture and ask what is happening, what happened before and what might happen next.

4. Build a tower

Use blocks or safe boxes. Count the pieces and discuss what makes a tower stable.

5. Sort by colour

Group safe objects by colour, then try sorting by size, shape or use.

6. Shape detective

Look for circles, squares, rectangles and triangles around the house.

7. Make a number game

Write numbers on paper and put them in order. Older children can create simple addition challenges.

8. Draw and describe

Ask your child to draw something they saw today and describe the picture.

9. Nature collection

During a safe outdoor walk, observe or collect leaves, stones or other safe natural objects and compare them.

10. Make a pattern

Create a pattern such as red-blue-red-blue and ask what comes next.

11. Memory game

Place five familiar objects on a table, let the child observe them, cover them and remove one. Ask what is missing.

12. Kitchen maths

During age-appropriate food preparation, count, compare and measure with adult supervision.

13. Create a mini book

Fold paper and create a cover, characters and a short story. Younger children can dictate their story.

14. Sound detective

Choose a sound and think of words beginning with it.

15. Build with recycled materials

Use clean cardboard boxes or paper rolls to make a house, bridge or other project.

16. Five-minute drawing challenge

Give a simple drawing challenge and add new instructions as the child works.

17. Feelings conversation

Ask what made the child happy, what was difficult and what helped. This builds emotional vocabulary and connection.

18. Daily routine chart

Create a simple chart with three to five daily tasks and let the child mark completed steps.

19. Family learning walk

Look for numbers, signs, colours, shapes, plants and sounds. Let the child lead some observations.

20. Family learning challenge

Choose one activity each day: Monday reading, Tuesday numbers, Wednesday creativity, Thursday nature, Friday life skills, Saturday family project and Sunday story and reflection.

You don't need expensive materials

Paper, pencils, bottle tops, clean containers, boxes, socks, leaves and stones can become learning materials when used safely and with a clear purpose.

Make room for screen-free learning

A balanced routine can include conversation, movement, outdoor play, reading and hands-on exploration. Instead of thinking only about removing screens, think about what meaningful activity will replace passive screen time.

Try one predictable 30–60 minute screen-free family session this week. Put devices aside, choose one activity and participate together.

Final message

Children need opportunities to ask, try, make mistakes, create, move, talk and explore. Put the screen down for a while and pick up a book, pencil, box, ball or simply a conversation.

You may already have everything you need for a meaningful learning moment.
